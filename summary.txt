World news — Wednesday, 11 November 2026

Two days of US-mediated talks between Ukraine, Russia and the United States ended in Abu Dhabi without a peace deal, but with agreement in principle on a prisoner exchange of about 1,000 people each side (Reuters). Ukraine rejected a US proposal covering unoccupied parts of Donbas. Ukraine said it hit oil tanks at Russia's Ust-Luga terminal; Russian strikes hit six substations and a Chornomorsk market, killing three and injuring 11.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said a tanker caught fire after striking a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz (AP). US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said pressure on Iran was working and major combat was no longer needed, but force remained possible if Tehran crossed red lines (New York Times). The US Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over about 20 alleged plots, including one at RAF Fairford.

Israeli forces killed at least seven people in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said; aid agencies said about 150,000 tents were stuck outside Gaza after storms flooded displaced people. Israeli strikes continued in southern Lebanon, and Saudi Arabia said it intercepted seven Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Riyadh.

At COP31 in Antalya, UN Secretary-General António Guterres told 102 heads of state that the climate crisis was "outrunning us" and urged timelines to phase out fossil fuels (AP). The United States stayed away for a second year, while Australia, Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria and Vanuatu backed a plan to double homes with rooftop solar by 2030.

In the US, the House voted 211-204 to end an effort to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth. Democrat Scott Colom beat Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi's Senate race by about 4,700 votes (Associated Press), the state's first Black senator since Reconstruction; Democrats now hold 55 Senate seats. US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, and the Nasdaq gained 1.6% on AI stocks.
